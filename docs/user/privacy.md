# Privacy notice

*Kind: policy · Audience: everyone who uses the hosted sites · Status: current (2026-10-01)*

This notice tells you which personal data the two hosted sites keep, and why. It
also tells you how to have that data corrected or removed. Personal data is any
information about a person who can be identified, for example an email address.

The two sites are:

- `le2.logicalcontracts.com`, the Logical English editor, and
- `lps2.logicalcontracts.com`, the Logic Production Systems (LPS) editor.

## Who is responsible

**Renting Point - Serviços de Informática, Lda.** runs the sites. This company is
registered in Portugal, and it operates the `logicalcontracts.com` domain. The
General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) of the European Union calls the company
the *controller*. The controller is the party that decides which personal data is
kept, and why.

For each question or request about your data, write to
**support@logicalcontracts.com**.

## The short version

- You can use the two sites without an account. You do not have to tell us who you
  are.
- If you sign in, we receive your email address. We receive no other information
  about you.
- We keep your email address only if you have a licence, or if we made a password
  account for you.
- We do not keep the programs that you write with your name or with your email
  address.
- We do not show advertising. We do not use tracking cookies. We do not sell or
  share data.

## Signing in

You can sign in with a Google account or a GitHub account. You can also sign in
with an email address and a password that we made for you.

When you sign in with Google or GitHub, the site sends you to Google or GitHub. You
sign in there. Then Google or GitHub tells us one thing: your email address. Google
or GitHub tells us the address only after it makes sure that the address is
really yours. We do not ask for your name, your photograph, your contacts or any
other information.

After you sign in, your browser keeps a small file with the name `lc_session`. This
type of file is a cookie. The cookie contains your email address and the method
that you used to sign in. With the cookie, the two sites know who you are. The
cookie expires after 14 days, or when you sign out.

During a sign-in with Google or GitHub, the browser keeps a second cookie,
`lc_oauth`. This cookie stays for a maximum of ten minutes. It makes sure that the
answer from Google or GitHub goes back to the browser that sent the request.

The sites themselves keep no information about a person who signs in with Google or
GitHub and who has no licence. The servers record that a sign-in occurred, and the
method of the sign-in. The servers do not record who signed in.

## What we keep, and why

| What | Why | How long |
|---|---|---|
| For a licence holder: the email address, the licence, its expiry date, and a reference to the invoice or agreement | To give the licence holder what the licence pays for | Until some months after the licence ends, or until you ask us to remove the data |
| For a password account: the email address and a scrambled form of the password (a *hash*). Nobody can get the password back from the hash | To let that person sign in | Until the account is closed, or until you ask us to remove the data |

We keep email addresses only in these two lists. The legal ground to keep the email
addresses is the licence agreement, or your own request for an account (GDPR
Article 6(1)(b)).

## Your programs

To answer a query, the server receives the program that you work on. The server
calculates the answer and sends the answer back to you. The server keeps the
program in its memory only while you use the program. The server does not keep the
program with your account or with your email address. Some features write a
working copy of the program to the temporary space of the server. Two examples are
the assistants and the importers of other systems. The server clears that space
when the server starts again.

## The assistants

Some features of the editors use a large language model to help you write Logical
English. A large language model is a program from another company that writes
text. When you use one of those features, the site sends your text for the
assistant to the company of the model that you selected. If the feature needs your
program, the site also sends your program. Examples of these companies are OpenAI,
Anthropic and Google. That company uses the text under its own terms. Do not give
the assistants personal data that you do not want that company to receive.

## Error reports and visit counts

When an error occurs on a site, the site sends a report of the error to **Sentry**.
Sentry is a service that collects error reports. The report contains the name of
the operation that failed and the error message. The report does not contain your
program, your query or your email address. The *Feedback* form also sends your
message to Sentry. The form sends a name and an email address only if you type
them.

The sites count visits with **Cloudflare Web Analytics**. Cloudflare Web Analytics
records which pages people visit, from which country, and with which type of
browser and device. Cloudflare Web Analytics does not set a cookie. It does not
follow you from one site to another site.

## Cookies

The sites use only the cookies that are necessary for what you asked for:

- `lc_session` and `lc_oauth`, for the sign-in, as described above;
- on the Logical English site, `le_ui_lang`. This cookie keeps the language that
  you selected for the menus.

The sites do not use these cookies for advertising or for tracking. Thus the sites
do not ask for your consent before they set these cookies.

## Other companies that handle data for us

- **Fly.io** runs the two servers, in London (United Kingdom). The European Union
  recognises that the United Kingdom gives adequate protection to personal data.
- **Google** and **GitHub**, only when you select to sign in with them.
- **Sentry** and **Cloudflare**, for error reports and visit counts.
- The company of the language model that you select, when you use an assistant.

Some of these companies can handle data outside the European Union, for example in
the United States. Each of these companies does this under its standard terms for
data protection. These terms include the safeguards that the GDPR requires for
such transfers.

## Your rights

At any time, you can write to us at **support@logicalcontracts.com** and ask us:

- to send you a copy of the data that we keep about you;
- to correct the data;
- to remove the data. For a licence holder, the removal of the email address ends
  the licence;
- to stop the use of the data.

We answer in one month or less. If our answer does not satisfy you, you can
complain to the Portuguese data protection authority, the *Comissão Nacional de
Proteção de Dados* (CNPD), at [www.cnpd.pt](https://www.cnpd.pt). You can also
complain to the authority of the country where you live.

## Changes to this notice

When this notice changes, the date at the top also changes. If a change has an
effect on what we keep about licence holders, we also send the change to them by
email.
